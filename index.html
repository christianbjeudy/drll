PHYSICIAN · EDUCATOR · ATHLETE

Care informed by movement and purpose.

Dr. Laurenie Louissaint is a board-certified physician in Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation, Assistant Professor of Sports Medicine, and Head Team Physician for the Haiti Women’s National Team.

CLINICAL APPROACH

Her work is centered on helping patients remain active, recover from musculoskeletal conditions, and return to the activities that matter to them.

ACADEMIC MEDICINE

At SUNY Downstate, she combines patient care with resident education, scholarship, and mentorship in sports and rehabilitation medicine.

AREAS OF EXPERTISE

Specialized care for active lives.

Clinical interests and services reflected across Dr. Louissaint’s current academic and hospital profiles.

01Sports MedicineNon-operative care supporting movement, recovery, and performance.+

Clinical interests include sports and overuse injuries, return-to-activity planning, and care organized around each patient’s movement goals.

  • Evaluation of sports, overuse, and activity-related concerns
  • Individualized rehabilitation and return-to-activity planning
  • Collaboration with therapists, trainers, and care teams

This approach connects careful diagnosis with practical recovery planning so active patients can move forward with confidence and clear next steps.

02Physical Medicine & RehabilitationFunction-focused care informed by rehabilitation medicine.+

A function-first approach to conditions affecting muscles, joints, nerves, and mobility, with an emphasis on independence and quality of life.

  • Assessment of muscles, joints, nerves, and mobility
  • Rehabilitation strategies designed around daily and athletic goals
  • Emphasis on independence, participation, and quality of life

Care is framed around the whole person, connecting medical findings to the movements, responsibilities, and activities that shape everyday life.

03Pain ManagementEvidence-informed evaluation and care for musculoskeletal pain.+

Thoughtful assessment of musculoskeletal pain using rehabilitation, procedural, and lifestyle strategies when appropriate.

  • Evaluation of musculoskeletal and activity-related pain
  • Rehabilitation, recovery, and lifestyle strategies
  • Selected procedure options when clinically appropriate

The goal is to understand how pain affects function, then build a balanced plan that supports sustainable progress rather than a one-size-fits-all response.

04Interventional SpineSpecialized training in spine care and image-guided procedures.+

Fellowship-informed evaluation of spine-related pain and selected image-guided treatment options.

  • Assessment of spine-related pain and function
  • Non-operative care planning informed by fellowship training
  • Selected image-guided treatment options when appropriate

A detailed history and physical examination help connect symptoms with mobility and function before treatment options are considered.

05Musculoskeletal UltrasoundUltrasound-supported assessment and procedural care.+

Real-time ultrasound may support soft-tissue and joint evaluation and help guide selected procedures with precision.

  • Real-time soft-tissue and joint visualization
  • Dynamic evaluation when movement matters
  • Guidance for selected procedures when clinically appropriate

Because ultrasound can show structures in motion, it can add useful context to an examination and support precise, patient-centered decision-making.

06Cycling & Running MedicineMovement-focused care for endurance athletes and active patients.+

Care for endurance athletes and active patients may include training-related concerns, biomechanics, recovery, and safe return to sport.

  • Training-related and overuse concerns
  • Biomechanics, recovery, and training-load considerations
  • Safe return to endurance activity and sport

Her perspective as a cyclist complements clinical training with an appreciation for endurance, consistency, equipment, and the realities of returning to sport.
